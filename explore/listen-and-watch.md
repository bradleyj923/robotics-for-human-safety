# Listen, watch, then ask a better question

The official episode pages and chapter outlines were reviewed for these six selections; this is a curated listening guide, not a claim to have watched every episode end to end. Our takeaways are discussion prompts, not quotations from the guests. These are starting points, not a requirement to consume hours of media. Pick one chapter, watch one demonstration, and bring a question back to the group.

## Six Lex Fridman conversations

Thank you to Lex Fridman for making room for these long, thoughtful conversations, and to the guests for sharing their work, curiosity and hard-earned experience. Their generosity gives students and lifelong learners a chance to hear how ambitious ideas are questioned, tested and built. The links below lead to the original conversations so you can explore them in full.

### Marc Raibert · Boston Dynamics and the Future of Robotics · Episode 412
**Start here for:** movement, balance, dexterity and ambitious engineering. Raibert founded Boston Dynamics. His conversation offers a bridge between the spectacle of a moving robot and the work required to make it capable. [Marc Raibert interview](https://lexfridman.com/marc-raibert/) · [Transcript](https://lexfridman.com/marc-raibert-transcript/)

**Listen for:** What do animals do effortlessly that machines struggle to do?

### Ayanna Howard · Human-Robot Interaction and Safety-Critical Systems
**Start here for:** safety, trust and robots that work with people. The episode explores assistive systems, extreme environments and ethical responsibility. [Ayanna Howard interview](https://lexfridman.com/ayanna-howard/)

**Useful chapters:** safety-critical algorithms at **20:03**; human-robot interaction at **57:11**; trust at **1:02:38**.

**Discuss:** When should a person trust a machine, and what information would make that trust earned?

### Rodney Brooks · Robotics · Episode 217
**Start here for:** the long view from a robotics pioneer and iRobot co-founder. [Rodney Brooks interview](https://lexfridman.com/rodney-brooks/)

**Useful chapters:** first robots at **7:33**; believing in the impossible at **1:21:57**; advice for young people at **2:23:21**.

**Discuss:** How do you separate an ambitious research direction from an unrealistic timeline?

### Simone Giertz · Inventive Engineering and Design · Episode 372
**Start here for:** creativity, imperfect prototypes and the pleasure of making something. The original episode title contains profanity. [Simone Giertz interview](https://lexfridman.com/simone-giertz/)

**Useful chapters:** creative process at **46:05**; education at **52:53**; difficult projects at **58:27**.

**Try:** Build a deliberately simple prototype that teaches you something, even if it is not yet useful.

### Kate Darling · Social Robots, Ethics and Privacy · Episode 329
**Start here for:** the human side of a machine that invites emotional attachment. The official outline connects the robot-animal analogy at **1:10:35**, data concerns at **1:23:38**, and robotics ethics at **2:09:36**. [Kate Darling interview](https://lexfridman.com/kate-darling-2/)

**Our takeaway:** Appearance and behavior can change how people relate to a device. That makes privacy and consent part of the product, not a policy added afterward. Bring this conversation to the companion-robot and familiar-voice questions above. What would an honest relationship with a machine look like?

### Russ Tedrake · Control, Dynamics and Touch · Episode 114
**Start here for:** the physical intelligence behind a convincing demonstration. Explore passive dynamic walking at **04:29**, simulation at **1:47:00**, home robotics at **2:00:33**, and touch at **2:20:42**. [Russ Tedrake interview](https://lexfridman.com/russ-tedrake/)

**Our takeaway:** Moving through a home means interacting with an uncertain physical world. Pair the walking discussion with exoskeletons and the touch discussion with an assistive gripper. What can a robot learn from contact that a camera cannot tell it?

## A visual counterpoint: coordinated humanoid movement

Unitree's own history describes its **WuBot** performance at the February 16, 2026 Spring Festival Gala, involving G1 and H2 humanoid robots. Its [official site](https://www.unitree.com/) and [company timeline](https://www.unitree.com/mobile/about/) provide a starting point for the performance and robot demonstrations.

Watch with two questions in mind: **What must be synchronized to make this work? What would change if the floor, lighting or task were unpredictable?** A choreographed performance is an invitation to study control and coordination, not a substitute for testing a robot in a rescue environment.

## Firefighting from the air: a manufacturer example

EHang describes its **EH216F** as an aerial firefighting platform developed for high-rise scenarios. Its official product and launch materials make this a concrete example to study, rather than an imagined future use of drones. [EHang firefighting platform](https://www.ehang.com/ehang216f/) · [Official launch video](https://www.ehang.com/video/show/349.html) · [Additional EHang clip](https://www.ehang.com/video/show/653.html)

**Watch for the whole system:** reaching an elevated fire is only the beginning. Payload, replenishment, positioning, wind, heat and coordination with crews determine whether the aerial capability is useful. The additional clip was supplied for this collection; that page did not open during review. The official launch video provides a separately verified manufacturer source.
